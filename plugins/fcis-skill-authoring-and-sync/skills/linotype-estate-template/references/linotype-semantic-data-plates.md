# Linotype semantic data plates — measured relief-print diagrams

**Named lesson:** `LINOTYPE-DATA-01`, *The diagram is data, not decoration* (19 November 2026).

**Scope:** Use for Fountain Coach architectural essays, domain-reasoning explanations, FCIS learning-cycle diagrams, line charts, and source-owned technical illustrations inside the existing Fountain Press Linotype composition. This reference extends `linotype-estate-template`; it does **not** create a new instrument, publisher, editor, graphic renderer, or authority. Its first local specimen is *Before We Can Work Together* with three SVG plates; the standalone PDF proof establishes only source/rendering geometry, not a staged or public Reader release.

## Why this is a learned procedure

A decorative picture of printing machinery or a page-shaped raster of arbitrary labels does not make an architecture intelligible. Historical Linotype casts type lines; diagrams are separate line blocks composed with the same type measure. Compose a **diagram of semantic relationships** for a letterpress page, rather than an illustration **about** Linotype machines. The lesson also generalizes the independently documented `semantic-illustration-ink-boundaries.md` correction: outer figure alignment says nothing about lettering inside a drawn rectangle.

**The editorial rule:** Intelligibility comes from continuity of thought, precise syntax and proportionate paragraphs, not increased whitespace. Do not invent frequent paragraph breaks, isolate lines as a substitute for explanation, or use diagrams as filler.

## LINOTYPE-DATA-02 — semantic containment, not inflated whitespace

**Writer correction, 19 November 2026.** The rejected *Before We Can Work Together* pre-web proofs demonstrated that generous line spacing, increased page count and isolated captions are not evidence of intelligibility. Treat prose, ink diagram, legend and caption as **nested semantic compositions**, each with an inside and an outside. Apply [the page-as-frame hierarchy](page-is-the-frame.md) before moving any element.

The **inside** of the plate belongs to its data: rectangles contain specimen type, internal rules relate meaningful entities, and text must be measured against the specific rectangle it represents. The **outside** of the plate belongs to the essay: a figure and its caption form one group, attached at its authored source location and separated from adjacent prose as a group. Keep caption adjacent to the figure and avoid duplicating source measurements both inside the plate and in the caption. Do not expand every gap independently or make labels into floating ornaments.

For a negative/positive comparison, test **four separate regions**: the intentionally failed *inner* test frame (overflow can be portrayed there and must be labelled as a negative), the *outer* illustrated plate (no ink may escape), the comparison panel next door (no collision), and the full folio type area (all figure ink and caption in measure). Reproduce a small, intelligible overflow specimen rather than letting the error contaminate the caption or the adjacent positive panel. Use one concise caption to state the actual measured evidence; keep the diagram conceptual/schematic where its geometry is not the actual measured source.

**Before claiming editorial correction:** compare original and revised *full-size* folio renders, verify unchanged paragraph boundaries and text, inspect inside/outside distances and caption attachment, and record that the editor's marked error is actually remedied. A mechanically valid PDF is not editorial acceptance, and a proof rejected by the writer does not become an accepted baseline.

## The reproducible procedure

1. **State the data question.** Describe the *one* relationship the plate must clarify, and identify the paragraph after which it belongs. Distinguish concept/specification, observed system status, and measured execution evidence. Do not translate proposals into apparently real receipts.
2. **Choose an ink vocabulary.** Use black lines, printer's rules, serif type, careful label insets, and solid/dashed line semantics with a compact legend. Prefer a monochrome source-owned SVG with meaningful entities and edges. No faux machinery, engraved façades, 3-D devices, gradients, decorative shadows, invented numbers or arrow ornaments.
3. **Compose the type.** Treat diagram labels as actual type with stable literal strings, font metrics and deliberate line breaks. Draw rectangles and connectors in source units. Keep outer frame aligned to the essay's Satzspiegel. Within each drawn rectangle independently measure *every* glyph bounding box against that rectangle, including internal minimum left/right/top/bottom clearances. Do not solve overflow by cropping, shrinking the whole composition, or substituting AI-generated pseudo-lettering.
4. **Preserve semantic branches.** When illustrating an FCIS learning cycle, show intention → inquiry → domain reasoning → distinct *Skill/procedure* and *instrument/capability* paths → test/verify → retain or authorize/admit → renewed inquiry. A Skill records a verified procedure; an instrument supplies the executed capability. A route line must connect, not float. Never depict authorization as automatic learning.
5. **Anchor the source and proof.** Keep image ID, exact source paragraph position, alt, precise caption, evidence status, rights, viewBox and source revision together. The first principal figure remains the source-owned hero only if authored and admitted as such; do not manufacture a social hero. Compose in the existing Fountain Press source-addressed `inFlowFigure` path with no second Reader.
6. **Verify both vector and folio.** Parse SVG source; ensure drawing/text semantics are legible when rasterized at actual single-leaf size; inspect isolated plate PNGs and the composed facing/single folios. Perform a negative glyph-overflow test and positive measured-clearance test in the existing browser harness (`getBBox()` per internal `text` and `rect`). Check caption attachment, ink alignment to Satzspiegel, readable label sizes, full source/prose continuity, long-label wrapping, mobile/iPad portrait and landscape, and large-text reflow. Classify local PDF, browser proof, real iPad, admitted staging, and production delivery independently.

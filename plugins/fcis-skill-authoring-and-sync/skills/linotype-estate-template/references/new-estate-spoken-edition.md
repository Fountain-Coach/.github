# Audio inside the *developing* unified Bookshelf — source and reader identity

This workflow applies when adding narrated text to an evolving Linotype Bookshelf or First Readings collection. Keep [the page-frame contract](page-is-the-frame.md), [reader navigation and Teatro Score composition](reader-navigation-and-score-composition.md), the owning source/Git revisions, and current stage state in force. A successful audio transport does **not** establish that it was attached to the correct editorial generation.

## Identify the work before installing a player

1. Inspect writer intent, active *developing* Bookshelf/Reader source, owning branch ancestry and current immutable WLAN stage symlink and served DOM. Follow the **actual** collection and internal volume identity. Do not choose a nearby historical standalone essay merely because it has the same title, a working public route or an easier HTML file. The October/November Teatro incident first attached audio to an obsolete essay edition: technically playable, editorially wrong. That wrong integration was reverted and its PR closed without merge.
2. Resolve the exact authored work revision and stable source-block identity in the **active new reader**. Reconstruct the spoken source text in order from its tagged title/prose blocks, excluding authenticated illustrations, image captions or other nonspeaking editorial furniture unless the manuscript explicitly includes them. Compare the resulting SHA-256 to the instrument-bound manuscript before showing a narrator button. A volume name or matching title is not proof of text identity. Preserve the original score and pagination.
3. Bind an authorized, digest-checked **recorded** edition through the reader's existing SatzfunkPlayer and stable private edition manifest. Check narrator/voice provenance, media/source SHA-256, rights and secure-origin permissions. Do not create a second HTML audio player, independent chapter URL, cloned continuous-text view, new page compositor or third-party media transport. A failure to admit audio must leave reading/pagination functional.
4. Put minimal Play/Pause/position transport in the existing **persistent book frame**, outside pagination leaves that are cloned, measured or rebuilt. Never autoplay, reset the session on a page turn, or bind a recording position to transient folio numbers. Only expose the control while its source work is active, pause and hide it on leaving the work, and make its AI-generated provenance accessible. Do not claim word/passage highlighting without independently established time alignment.
5. Use the current owner HTTPS WLAN preview for private media, and never copy its private manifest or raw recording into the public/static source tree. Do not conflate a Reframe Bookshelf membership with public rights. A contributor's artistically accepted voice is a separate claim from an independently transcript-aligned master.

## Independent acceptance that protects the work

- Confirm the internal navigation **Bookshelf → collection → actual work**; the fact that a legacy edition deep link renders is not enough. Read back exact served source/manifest/audio identities and the immutable active release.
- In browsers with meaningful differences, exercise responsive folio layout, book-page turns, active/hidden transport, actual advancing playback, and leaving the work. Use Chromium and WebKit where possible. For physical iPad Safari, touch, keyboard, screen reader or VoiceOver, report only what was actually witnessed.
- Do not invent a failure when the WAV and MP3 report slightly different durations. In the measured Vergil case, Chromium reported approximately 498.970 seconds and WebKit approximately 499.032 seconds due to MP3 encoder padding. Compare within a *narrow, recorded codec tolerance* and show a rounded 8:19 duration; don't assert byte-for-byte duration equality or fake source timing.
- HTTP 200 and the correct MP3 SHA-256 prove served bytes, **not** that native seeking, accessibility or playback through page turns works. Where the existing WLAN server omits byte-range support, diagnose actual seeking before changing the player or server. Prefer the smallest justified, source-owned transport adaptation and separately verify a seek effect. Do not create another service or player to hide the defect.
- Preserve the active new-estate staging reader, all unrelated source/gallery content and prior stage rollback. Never copy an older public version over later accepted stage-only Bookshelf developments.

## Provenance and exclusions

The source-grounded *Teatro — An Unfinished Composition* case was integrated into the **new** `Fountain-Coach/fountain.coach` Bookshelf development line through [PR #6](https://github.com/Fountain-Coach/fountain.coach/pull/6). Its 29 source title/prose blocks matched the renderer's authoritative manuscript digest. The original SatzfunkPlayer continued through page turns; leaving Teatro paused/hid the control. Live private HTTPS desktop/phone Chromium + WebKit acceptance passed four cases without reported page errors. The mistaken legacy edition [PR #5](https://github.com/Fountain-Coach/fountain.coach/pull/5) closed without merge; its accidental staged controls were reverted. The source-native audio correction is owned by the separate Swift Kit [PR #85](https://github.com/Fountain-Coach/midi2-gpu-fabric/pull/85).

These are source and **private browser** witnesses, not publication consent, universal accessibility proof, native iPad/VoiceOver acceptance or full Reframe Store/AX live acceptance.
